Boxes, glue, penalties

A paragraph becomes a list of three things. Boxes are words, and are rigid. Glue is the space between them, which may stretch or shrink within limits. Penalties mark places a line could end, and what it would cost: a hyphen is allowed, but not free.

A shortest path

Every possible line is scored by how far its glue must move from rest, cubed, so one badly spaced line costs more than several slightly imperfect ones. The best paragraph is then the cheapest route from the first word to the last, found by dynamic programming in close to linear time.

Small courtesies

Hyphenation follows Liang’s patterns, as TeX’s does. Two hyphenated lines in a row are discouraged, as is a tight line beside a loose one. Full stops, commas and quotation marks hang slightly into the margin, so the edge of the text looks straight rather than merely being straight.
